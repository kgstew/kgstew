### 5. Management: what do you believe that others don't?

You've managed humans and you list it second in your own bio, but the site has one
sentence about it. What do you actually believe about managing people that not everyone
agrees with?

A specific moment lands harder than a principle — a hire you fought for, a team you
restructured, a call you got wrong and what it taught you. Volunteer crews count; running
Fable Bound's crew for eleven months is management, and unpaid people are harder.

**A:**

People are capable of a lot more than we tend to give them credit for as managers. This especially comes out in volunteerism and in working on large art projects, where people come in not necessarily having the required skills to do a particular thing, but they're very interested in the work and it's something that they want to accomplish.

Working with people to help them learn the skills that they need and also encourage them to not just skill up but also take on leadership positions and take ownership for the work that they're doing is critical in training employees, both in a volunteer sense and a professional sense. I think ownership, agency, trusting people, encouraging them to do their best work, and not micromanaging them has been a great part of my success as a manager.

I also think that because I have a broad set of skills and understandings, it makes it easy for me to digest and explain concepts between different groups of people. This helps to bridge the gap between technical and non-technical and help find opportunities for people to learn things that are outside of their immediate skill set. This is because I understand what they are strong in and what those existing skill sets and those existing capabilities enable them to do and to learn. I can point them in a direction to help them grow.

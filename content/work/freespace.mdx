freespace began in June 2013 when a building was gifted to the community for a dollar for the
month. The barrier to entry was set deliberately at nothing, and the door stayed open, which is why
the people who turned up had almost nothing in common except wanting to make something.

I held the lease on the building and was one of the people running it day to day — the original
space in the summer of 2013, and again in a different building the following summer.

An empty, underused space in the middle of San Francisco became a cultural movement in the city,
and then twelve other spaces elsewhere built on the same model.
